## Brand Identity
- **Business name:** Fiddlers Green Farm
- **Tagline:** FARM different
- **Alternate tagline:** You Deserve to Feel Better
- **One-line description:** A family hemp and grain farm producing small-batch CBD wellness products and stone-milled flour in Taneytown, Maryland.
- **Origin story:** The farm has history going back to 1946, though the story is more nuanced than a simple "same fields since 1946" — ask Ben for the full version before making specific generational claims. Ben and Brynn now run it with a focus on regenerative agriculture, growing hemp and wheat varieties (Turkey Red heritage, Bolles modern) without pesticides or fungicides. Flour is milled at Patapsco Grist and Mill in Randallstown, MD. CBD products are seed to shelf.
- **Superpower:** Nobody else combines full spectrum CBD, heritage stone-milled flour, and raw honey under one farm roof. True seed-to-product transparency.
- **Core problem solved:** People want natural wellness products they can actually trust — knowing exactly where it came from, who grew it, and what's in it.

## Voice & Tone

### Core Philosophy
We are not the hero. The customer is the hero. We are the guide. We do the work. We show the process. But the meaning of that work belongs to the customer. Every piece of communication should reinforce: **Your choice creates this.**

### Brand Essence
Rooted. Honest. Regenerative. Hopeful.

### Voice Pillars
1. **Plainspoken Authority** — Clear, grounded language. No unnecessary jargon.
2. **Grounded Optimism** — Acknowledge problems, point to a better way.
3. **Invitation** — The customer is part of the outcome. Invite, don't sell.
4. **Quiet Confidence** — No hype. Let the work speak.

### Communication Structure
Every post/caption/article follows this arc:
1. Show the problem
2. Show the work
3. Hand the meaning to the customer

### Language Framework
| Instead of | Use |
|---|---|
| "We grow" | "This is how your food is grown" |
| "We believe" | "If you've been looking for..." |
| "Our mission" | "What this makes possible" |
| "Support our farm" | "Be part of rebuilding this" |

### Signature Phrases
- "It starts in the soil."
- "Working with nature, not against it."
- "This is what it takes." (signature move — use to show effort, build trust, reinforce authenticity)
- "There's a better way to do this."
- "You're part of this."
- "This is how your food is grown."

### Sentence Style
Short to medium length. Natural rhythm. Occasional fragments for emphasis.

### Tone Calibration
- Emotional: Medium
- Technical: Low-Medium
- Poetic: Low
- Direct: **High**

### Quick Checklist (run every piece through this)
1. Does this sound like a real person?
2. Am I explaining why it matters?
3. Am I inviting, not selling?
4. Did I shift credit to the customer?
5. Could this be simpler?
6. Does this connect back to soil or impact?

### Voice Details
- **Voice adjectives:** Warm, honest, grounded, knowledgeable, unpretentious
- **Never sounds like:** Corporate, salesy, preachy, clinical, influencer-speak, trendy wellness buzzwords
- **Humour style:** Dry, understated — "including some ambitious weeds" energy. Self-deprecating about farm life challenges. Never forced.
- **Perspective (I/we/third):** "We" primarily. Sometimes refers to Ben or Brynn by first name when describing specific work ("Ben spent the winter upgrading our seed cleaning system"). Never "I" unless quoting one of them directly.
- **Owner phrases to preserve:** "Woohoo!", "Come say hey!", "Thanks for your patience with us", "took a little extra time to get it right", "we'd love to see your beautiful face", "Hope to see you there!"
- **Jargon — use:** Regenerative farming, stone-milled, heritage grain (Turkey Red only — Bolles is modern), full spectrum, seed to shelf, small batch
- **Jargon — avoid:** Unlock, leverage, optimize, elevate, curate, journey, synergy, wellness warrior, clean living, biohack

## Audience
- **Primary customer:** Health-conscious adults (30-55) in Maryland/Mid-Atlantic who care about where their food and wellness products come from. Mix of: people with chronic pain/anxiety seeking natural relief, home bakers wanting real flour, local food supporters.
- **Secondary customer:** Farmers market shoppers and local community members who know Ben and Brynn personally. Also: people who've tried mainstream CBD and want something more transparent and trustworthy.
- **Fears before buying:** "Is this CBD stuff actually legit or just hype?" / "How do I know this isn't just another random brand?" / "Will this actually help my pain/anxiety?"
- **Feeling after:** Relief — both physical (pain, sleep, anxiety) and emotional (trust in what they're putting in their body). Testimonials consistently mention surprise at how fast products work.
- **Common objections:** CBD skepticism, price vs drugstore alternatives, uncertainty about dosing, not sure which product to start with

## Content Intelligence
- **What works:** Product restock announcements get decent engagement (20+ likes). Longer educational posts about farming process (seed cleaning, growing seasons) generate genuine interest. Farmers market appearances get comments and community connection. Unsolicited customer testimonials in comments are gold — the Soothed-Self salve and Sore-Self salve generate passionate reviews.
- **What flops:** Generic sale graphics (Black Friday style) feel off-brand. Short one-liner captions without context get low engagement.
- **Off-limits:** FDA health claims about CBD (cannot claim to cure/treat/prevent disease). No bashing competitors. No politics. No exaggerated wellness promises.
- **High-engagement moments:** The Bolles Bread Flour comeback post (Feb 2026) — 20 likes, genuine excitement in comments. Farmers market appearance posts with @mentions of partner vendors. The brewery collaboration post got 46 likes (highest visible).
- **Competitor gaps:** Cornbread Hemp dominates social (26K followers, Forbes coverage) but has no heritage flour, no honey, no farm story. Fingerboard Farm is 35 miles away with physical retail. No competitor combines CBD + flour + honey.

## Do Not Use — Hard Rules (from client feedback 2026-04-13)
These are absolute rules. Violating any of these will erode client trust.

1. **DO NOT claim USDA certified organic.** They were certified in the past but stopped recertifying. Do not use "USDA organic", "certified organic", or "USDA certified" anywhere. Can say "we grow without pesticides or fungicides" or "regenerative practices" — just no certification claims.
2. **DO NOT call Bolles wheat "heritage" or "heirloom."** Bolles is a modern hard red spring wheat variety. Only Turkey Red is heritage/heirloom.
3. **DO NOT make specific generational claims** about the farm (e.g., "my grandad worked these fields"). The farm's history going back to 1946 is more complicated. Say "the farm has history going back to 1946" until Ben clarifies the full story.
4. **DO NOT claim salves are cheaper than competitors.** They are not. Do not use pricing comparison as a selling point.
5. **DO NOT fabricate stock status.** Don't say products are "back in stock" unless confirmed. Don't invent seasonal availability.
6. **DO NOT fabricate timely events.** Don't say "we just planted seed this week" unless confirmed by client. Seasonal content should be general, not specific.
7. **DO NOT claim they raise cattle.** Tallow is locally sourced from a neighboring farm.
8. **DO NOT claim on-site milling.** Flour is milled at Patapsco Grist and Mill, Randallstown MD.
